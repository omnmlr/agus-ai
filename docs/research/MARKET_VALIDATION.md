# Market evidence and validation

## Three established purchasing categories

1. **Mobile testing capacity.** AWS publishes a metered real-device price of $0.17/device-minute [R1]. BrowserStack markets a large real-device cloud and subscription testing [R2]. Buyers already purchase access and orchestration rather than own every device.
2. **Real human testing.** Applause offers managed testing across devices, regions and payment conditions [R3]. Testlio sells managed payments testing and documents a customer example involving ten countries and a bank-account format issue [R4–R5]. These are vendor claims, not independent audits; they establish available products and plausible recurring buyer needs.
3. **Agent training and evaluation.** Scale markets GUI trajectories, evaluations and RL environments for licensing [R6]. AndroidControl research reports 14,548 unique tasks across 833 apps [R7]. This is research demand and commercial product evidence, not an AGUS contract.

## Jobs to be done

| Buyer | Pain | Existing substitute | AGUS hypothesis | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small Solana dApp team | No representative wallet/device matrix | Own phone, emulator, friends | Self-serve release campaign | Independent findings, reproducible runs |
| Mature dApp team | Regressions and costly manual release work | Internal QA, device farms | Repeatable wallet and user testing | Incremental defects vs current suite |
| Wallet/SDK team | Integration failures across app and OS versions | Issue triage, internal lab | Repro matrix and evidence | Reproduction rate, time saved |
| Ecosystem operator | Fragmented quality signals | Developer support, store review | Consent-based aggregated compatibility signals | Coverage, privacy, useful actionable trends |
| Agent developer | UI success claims don't prove outcomes | Benchmarks, internal data | Chain-grounded evaluations | Objective outcome labels, reproducibility |
| AI data buyer | Expensive, inconsistent demonstrations | Existing data vendors | Qualified, rights-cleared mobile trajectories | Acceptance rate, annotation quality, data rights |

## What this does not establish

No interviews or prospective-customer outreach were commissioned for this hackathon. We have not measured market share, buyer budgets, contracted volume, retention, willingness to pay, or cost of acquisition. Do not describe the three markets as a summed TAM: they overlap and AGUS addresses only subsets.

## Bottom-up opportunity model

Use active eligible teams × campaigns per team per year × average net campaign bill. Illustrative (not forecast): 100 teams × 4 campaigns × $150 = $60,000 annual gross billings; at a 25% platform share that is $15,000 before platform costs. A larger scenario, 500 × 6 × $300 = $900,000 gross, $225,000 platform share. Neither number is inferred from Solana's developer count. This is sensitivity analysis; actual utilization and pricing determine viability.

## Post-hackathon validation (not a current prerequisite)

Offer scoped trials to teams with a real release or reproducible issue. Measure:
- Paid conversion after a useful initial report; repeat bookings within 90 days.
- Incremental defects and severity, reproduction success, time to actionable result.
- Contribution margin by workload and device class, fill rate, time to match, cancellation and support cost.
- Buyer acceptance of evidence, privacy terms and datasets.
- Provider effective hourly earnings including qualification, waiting and rework.

A weak signal would be sign-ups without jobs, reports without new information, or paying customers who do not repeat. Kill or narrow a product line if repeated pilots cannot deliver value at a sustainable price.
